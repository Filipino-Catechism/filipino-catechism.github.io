01Art CompetitionSaints in Every Stroke: Draw Faith, Draw Hope!

Your class saint, in your own artwork

Depict your class’s assigned saint, their symbols and stories. Everyone in class can participate and submit. Your catechist will choose the top three artworks, and all three will be displayed for each section.

Format & materials

  • A4 paper, portrait orientation.
  • Pencil, coloured pencil, crayons or watercolour.
  • Digital art is not allowed.

Age categories

  • Category 1: KG1, KG2, Level 1 & Level 2.
  • Category 2: Levels 3, 4, 5 & 6.
  • Category 3: Levels 7, 8, 9 & 10.

Rules

  • Be original: no copying or tracing. Use your own interpretation.
  • Stay on theme: clearly connect the artwork to the saint and their symbols.
  • Present it neatly: no folds, smudges or distracting borders.

03All Saints Day ParadeFriday, 6 November · Batch timings to be announced

Class guidelines

All class members should participate. Catechists and students can decide how their class will dress for the parade.

  • Wear a particular colour or your class T-shirt, if available.
  • Dress as nuns, priests or angels.
  • Wear various saint costumes for the parade; these are not part of the official competition unless worn by the designated representatives.

Your class banner or placard

  • Name of the saint.
  • Patron saint of, or what the saint is known for.
  • One-line attribute, virtue or quotation associated with the saint.

Class representative guidelines

Each section has one pair of representatives: one male and one female, portraying their assigned saints.

Submit class representative names by Friday, 23 October 2026.

Each representative must wear a sash bearing the saint’s name and prepare a short introduction of 30–40 seconds maximum. It may be read from notes or delivered from memory.

The introduction may include

  • The saint’s name and patronage, or what they are known for.
  • A key virtue or characteristic.
  • A meaningful quote.
  • A brief explanation of the symbols or props used in the portrayal.

Simple costumes, joyful participation

Families are encouraged to create costumes with simplicity, creativity and care. Avoid costly or tailor-made outfits and use materials available at home whenever possible.

Let this activity teach frugality, humility, creativity and joyful simplicity, following the saints’ example. Participants are judged on saintly and joyful behaviour, knowledge, portrayal, creativity and simplicity—not on the cost or elaborateness of a costume.

YOUR PARADE PLAN

Three parade-day priorities

Stay with your class

Follow your teacher and class leader. Let them know if you need a break.

Be kind & make space

Walk calmly, be gentle with other people’s costumes and look out for each other.

Keep comfortable

Bring your water bottle and tell an adult if you feel tired or unwell.

Route & meeting points will be confirmed

The 2026 parade route, class order and assembly points have not yet been supplied. Your teacher or event team will share the final directions. Check classroom guidance.

These are general family preparation tips. Final event mechanics and safety instructions will come from the organizing team.

DRESS WITH JOY

A costume with a story

Your costume helps you tell the story of your assigned saint. It can be simple, thoughtful and comfortable.

  • Confirm your assigned saint with your teacher before making a costume.
  • Ask a parent or guardian to help. Choose clothes and shoes you can walk in easily.
  • Keep props light and safe. Ask your teacher about any prop restrictions.
  • Help students learn a fact about their saint and the virtue they represent.
Meet the saints

Safety & supervision

If you can’t find your group, stop and ask a teacher or event volunteer for help. Stay inside the parish event area and tell a trusted adult where you are.

If you feel unwell or need urgent help, tell a nearby responsible adult immediately.
